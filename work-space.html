The gallery

Akasha, the ether, the fifth element, the one that contains all others; Kū, the Japanese void, not absence but pure potentiality. Yellow floats in this metaphysical between-space. Above a blue-tinged Earth, he waves with candid gesture at an immensity that sovereignly ignores him. The Moon, disproportionate, occupies nearly as much space as the distant Sun — a masterful optical illusion that interrogates our relationship to proximity. What appears close rarely is. What appears distant governs us. A satellite crosses the frame, a discreet reminder that even Akasha, once pristine, now bears our technological fingerprints.
